## Verdict

The core holds up:

- The lock rules never overwrite an ID.
- All five proof-of-possession bindings are verified on the host against a fresh nonce.
- Secrets are zeroized, key files are written 0600 atomically, and p11-fault's unsafe code is sound.
- Every numbered 2026-11-18 finding is fixed or decided, except one that is fixed only partly (C-14). Spot checks found no regressions.

The weaknesses are at the edges:

- **Guards that recognise the wrong thing:** the YubiKey scratch slot (D-1) and the offline transport file (C-1).
- **Attestation that is not tied to the key's material:** B-1, B-2 and A-3.
- **Probe rows counted as "works" without checking the result:** D-2 to D-5, plus A-1.
- **CLI drift across the three tools.**

On complexity, roughly 1.7–2k lines of Rust and Nix and about 1,060 lines of live documentation can go without losing behaviour. The biggest single item is a provider seam that no provider implements.

**Counts:** 1 critical, 2 high, 30 medium, 59 low (92 findings; about 85 once the duplicates below are merged).

**Re-checked against the code by the lead:** D-1, B-1, C-1, A-2, A-4, D-6.

- **A-11 = B-7.** The 78-byte proof challenge does not fit OAEP-SHA256 on RSA-1024, and the failure surfaces as 16 (unavailable). Fix it once, in `pop.rs`, as B-7 proposes: encrypt a 32-byte secret.
- **C-17 ⊂ A-6.** Vendor-method planning has no producer. Delete it while moving camigrate's model out of core.
- **D-1, D-22, D-19.** One fix covers all three: a probe-specific slot marker, a check of `scratchId` against the token's locators and the retired-slot range, and the two missing guard tests.
- **B-9 and D-18.** Provider hints are appended to every `Unsupported`, and from there they reach committed profile evidence. Attach hints per cause, and keep prose out of evidence.
- **B-6, B-15 and A's cross-note.** p11-names has one user, and the `rv-value` feature exists because nothing else maps names to values. Let pkcs11 use p11-names and drop the feature.

## CLI surface (detail and the full table: [C.md, "CLI surface"](REVIEW-2026-11-23/C.md))

Problems:

- `--token` means the source, the target, an address filter or the token measured, depending on the verb.
- "Don't write" is spelled three ways: `--no-update-lock`, `--dry-run`, and leaving out `--apply`.
- Some flags do nothing or are silently ignored: `--update-lock`; `export --passphrase-file` for a bundle; `--transport-key` with a file; `--json` on a real export; `--rsa-max-bits` without `--write`.
- `verify` is an alias of `check`, but README and ARCHITECTURE document it as its own verb.
- `--json` exists in one tool only.
- `import --in` picks its parser by whether `--passphrase-file` is present.
- Notes are buffered until exit, so a killed `migrate` prints nothing.
- The three `--help` exit-code lists disagree, and cakeys' list is missing 21.
- Help text carries jargon and design IDs ("Pseudo-FOD", "D3", "D11", and "SO" in two meanings).
- caprobe's `--layer` help names a layer that does not exist (D-7).
- The regeneration command written into every profile omits `--rsa-max-bits 4096`, so following it breaks the conformance test (D-6).

Proposed surface:

- An object address is `<object>…` plus repeatable `--spec`. `--token` always means where the object is, never a source or a target.
- camigrate states direction with `--from`/`--to` on `export`, `import` and `migrate`, and the destination kind with `--to-transport-file` or `--to-key-file`.
- Every verb that writes takes `--dry-run`.
- One global `--json` for report output.
- Notes stream to stderr.
- One exit-code table, rendered into every tool's `--help`.
- Drop `--update-lock` and the `verify` alias.
- Rename `caprobe probe --write` to `--write-probes`.
